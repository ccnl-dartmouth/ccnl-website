The real world’s volatility and uncertainty demand flexible decision making that relies on adopting appropriate valuation strategy and combination of information. In contrast, standard models of choice under risk and uncertainty assume that reward probability and magnitude, or transformations of them, are multiplied to compute the subjective value of risky options. This formulation, however, limits flexibility when these attributes differ in uncertainty and therefore need to be weighted differently. A so-called **additive model** offers an alternative by directly comparing and differentially weighting reward attributes to guide choice, without first integrating them into a single subjective value. We have validated this hypothesis through multiple studies across species [@2019-spitmaan-salience-driven-value, @2018-farashahi-flexibility-basic-risk, @2019-farashahi-flexible-combination-reward, @~lewis-in-prep, @~yazdanpanah-in-prep].

In both humans and monkeys, we observed flexible decision-making, with humans weighting reward magnitude or probability and monkeys adjusting risk preferences based on task context [@2019-spitmaan-salience-driven-value, @2018-farashahi-flexibility-basic-risk, @2019-farashahi-flexible-combination-reward, @~lewis-in-prep, @~cochran-in-prep]. Furthermore, whereas a **multiplicative strategy** dominates under risk, both humans and monkeys adopt an additive strategy under uncertainty, allowing for more flexible integration of reward information [@2019-farashahi-flexible-combination-reward]. This flexibility is also reflected in prefrontal representations of reward magnitude [@2019-farashahi-flexible-combination-reward]. More recently, we have combined reinforcement learning and machine-learning approaches to uncover population-level mechanisms that support the flexible weighting of different learning systems during choice [@~woo-in-prep].

Finally, considering the brain as an integrated system is crucial for understanding adaptive behavior, which involves multiple cognitive processes and brain areas. Although the effects of reward on choice have been extensively studied, how reward interacts with sensory integration, attention, working memory, and other cognitive processes is less understood. Our research has examined: (1) how time pressure shapes the integration of sensory and reward information; (2) whether reward influences perceptual choice through sensory processing, decision making, or both; and (3) how these interactions contribute to robustness against neural perturbations [@2018-farashahi-dynamic-combination-sensory, @2020-rakhshan-influence-expected-reward, @2026-shires-working-memory-impervious, @2025-rakhshan-neural-mechanisms-underlying, @2021-soltani-separable-influences-reward].
