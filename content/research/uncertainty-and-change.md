A key challenge in adaptive learning is balancing adaptability in volatile environments with precise reward estimation, the **adaptability-precision tradeoff** [@2019-soltani-adaptive-learning-expected, @2017-farashahi-metaplasticity-as-neural, @2017-khorsand-optimal-structure-metaplasticity, @~lisitsyn-in-prep]. We previously proposed reward-dependent metaplasticity as a synaptic mechanism for integrating reward under uncertainty and tracking that uncertainty [@2017-farashahi-metaplasticity-as-neural]. A metaplastic model successfully performed probabilistic reversal learning by dynamically adjusting learning from feedback, with activity changes signaling volatility, and predicted time- and choice-dependent learning rates that we validated in non-human primates [@2017-farashahi-metaplasticity-as-neural].

Learning in naturalistic settings is also challenging because environments change across multiple timescales, including shifts in action-outcome relationships and reward probabilities [@2021-soltani-timescales-cognition-brain, @2022-soltani-computational-models-adaptive]. These changes create different forms of uncertainty that animals must navigate through their choices [@2019-stolyarova-contributions-anterior-cingulate, @2024-aguirre-dissociable-contributions-basolateral, @2026-aguirre-sex-differences-task], suggesting that adaptive behavior can be studied by linking uncertainty in the environment to uncertainty in choice behavior. To this end, we have developed several **information theory-based metrics** [@2021-trepka-entropy-based-metrics, @2025-woo-information-theoretic-framework] to quantify this link and applied them across species to study interactions between learning systems [@2021-trepka-entropy-based-metrics, @2025-woo-information-theoretic-framework, @2023-woo-mechanisms-adjustments-different, @~nong-in-prep, @2025-woo-contribution-amygdala-dynamic].

Uncertainty is typically divided into two types: expected uncertainty, arising from the inherent randomness of reward outcomes, and unexpected uncertainty, reflecting actual changes in the environment [@2019-soltani-adaptive-learning-expected]. This distinction, however, is closely linked to the timescales over which the brain responds to and integrates environmental events. To study this, we developed a comprehensive method for estimating **neural timescales** at the single-neuron level [@2020-spitmaan-multiple-timescales-neural]. We have shown that these timescales provide insight into diverse cognitive functions [@2021-soltani-timescales-cognition-brain, @2024-trepka-training-dependent-gradients, @2024-song-hierarchical-gradient-timescales], including learning under uncertainty [@2020-spitmaan-multiple-timescales-neural, @~spitmaan-in-prep].

Finally, a critical component of intelligence and adaptive behavior is learning from the experiences and opinions of others, or **social learning**. Social learning can accelerate reward learning without the costs of direct decision making and trial-and-error experience [@2020-soltani-learning-others-but]. We are investigating the similarities and differences between experiential and social learning, and their underlying neural mechanisms [@2026-benistant-belief-updating-social, @~duggins-in-prep, @2026-yazdanpanah-social-information-creates].
