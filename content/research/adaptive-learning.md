Adaptive behavior requires rapid learning after genuine environmental change, but resistance to ordinary variability in outcomes. How the brain distinguishes these two forms of uncertainty, and how it sets the extent of updating, remains unresolved, as does the degree to which species share these strategies. We study learning in foraging and reversal tasks in mice, monkeys and humans, in which the better option changes without warning. Choices are analyzed with reinforcement-learning models and with model-free, information-theoretic measures of how choice responds to reward.
