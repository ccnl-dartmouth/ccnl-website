Objects and actions in the real world have many features, and only some features predict reward, making learning about each feature separately an inefficient process. How learners select informative features, when they shift to learning about whole objects, and how attention guides this process are all active areas of research. We use behavioral experiments to study how human participants choose among stimuli differing in shape, color, pattern or naturalistic content, with periodic reward-probability estimates. We fit feature-based, object-based and mixed reinforcement-learning models to choices, complementing behavioral analysis with gaze measurements and trained recurrent neural networks.
